How COVID-19 Reshaped Retail Spending Across Canada

Visit at: https://syed-m-omar.github.io/Retail-Sales-Impacted-by-Covid-Across-Canada/ 

Overview

This project analyzes Canadian retail sales and Consumer Price Index (CPI) data sourced directly from Statistics Canada to examine how the COVID-19 pandemic affected retail activity across Canada.

Using SQL, the project investigates sales trends before, during, and after the pandemic while exploring provincial recovery patterns, inflation trends, and inflation-adjusted economic growth.

To present findings in an accessible and interactive format, the analysis was transformed into a responsive website built with HTML and CSS and deployed using GitHub Pages.

Project Objectives
Analyze Canadian retail sales before, during, and after COVID-19.
Compare retail performance across Canadian provinces and territories.
Identify the strongest post-pandemic recoveries.
Measure the severity of retail disruptions during the pandemic.
Examine inflation trends through Consumer Price Index (CPI) data.
Compare food inflation against overall inflation.
Calculate inflation-adjusted (real) retail growth.

Key Findings
Retail Sales Growth

Canadian retail sales increased by 19.1% following the pandemic, indicating strong recovery and continued consumer spending across the country.

Inflation Growth

Canada's Consumer Price Index increased by 11.1% over the same period, reflecting widespread increases in the cost of goods and services.

Real Retail Growth

After adjusting for inflation, retail sales experienced approximately 7.2% real growth, demonstrating that genuine economic expansion occurred beyond rising prices alone.

Strongest Provincial Recoveries

The provinces that experienced the strongest post-pandemic retail recoveries were:

Prince Edward Island (+28.8%)
Quebec (+24.2%)
Saskatchewan (+23.2%)
Hardest Hit Provinces

Quebec and Ontario experienced some of the largest retail sales declines during the initial pandemic shock, illustrating the severity of lockdown-related economic disruptions.

Inflation Trends

Food prices rose faster than overall inflation:

Food CPI: 13.9%
All-Items CPI: 11.1%

Food inflation also exceeded overall inflation throughout much of 2022, placing additional pressure on Canadian households.

Conclusion

The COVID-19 pandemic produced significant disruptions across Canada's retail sector, resulting in sharp short-term declines, varying provincial recovery patterns, and elevated inflationary pressures. While retail sales ultimately surpassed pre-pandemic levels, inflation accounted for a substantial portion of that growth.

By combining retail sales and CPI data, this project demonstrates that Canada's retail sector experienced genuine post-pandemic recovery. However, inflation-adjusted analysis revealed that real growth was considerably smaller than headline retail sales figures suggested, emphasizing the importance of evaluating both spending and price changes when assessing economic performance.
